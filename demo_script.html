Groundhog Loop

A retro follow-up agent that notices when the team has agreed to fix the same thing for the third time — and says so, loudly, instead of filing a third unrelated ticket.

The problem

Every retro produces action items. They get spoken, written down, and quietly dropped. That part is ordinary.

The expensive part is the second and third time. The same root cause comes back worded differently — "the ETL job doesn't alert", then "the staging sync times out", then "the warehouse batch can't be checkpointed" — and because no two of those strings match, it is re-triaged from scratch as a new problem instead of escalated as a systemic one.

A human in the room sometimes catches it. In the sample transcript, Marcus does: "Again? Didn't we talk about this before?" Nobody can answer him, because the evidence is spread across three meetings and two ticket systems.

Groundhog Loop answers him. It files the ticket and carries the thread: this is occurrence four, here are the three earlier tickets, here is the sentence in each meeting that proves it.

How the recurrence check works

Matching the same problem across differently-worded action items is the whole product, and it is the part that took the most measurement to get right. Six decisions carry it:

Two stages, two different jobs

Embeddings do recall at a deliberately loose cutoff; the model does precision. Neither stage is asked to do the other's job.

No single threshold can work — measured

On real vectors the weakest genuine cross-meeting pair scores 0.2425 and the strongest unrelated pair scores 0.4121. The bands overlap, so no cutoff separates them. Recall sits at 0.15 and lets the model judge.

The evidence is in the dialogue

The sentence that proves a repeat — "this is the third time … August retro, sprint 42, now this" — sits several lines from the action item and is otherwise thrown away. Extraction captures it whole, as a separate field. Without it, the grouping collapses.

A recurrence came back later

Same-meeting candidates are excluded. Two action items from one retro about one system are two tasks, not a recurrence — and same-meeting pairs are the strongest cosine matches in the sample, so they would dominate the shortlist.

A link merges threads

A new occurrence is often the first evidence that two separately tracked threads are one problem, so every group among the confirmed matches is merged. Picking only the best-scoring one orphans items and under-counts the thread.

Judgement failure fails closed

If adjudication errors out, the item is treated as new. Falling back to the cosine score would mean trusting a threshold already known not to separate.

The same run on anthropic/claude-sonnet-5 produces the same grouping at roughly ten times the cost and twice the wall time — the swap is one line in .env and no code change. Both are graded by the same regression test, which is how the comparison is a measurement rather than a preference.

Before you hit record

  • Preflight. .venv/bin/python test_grouping.py — exit 0 means safe to record. ~40s, no Jira issue, no Slack message, database untouched. A pass an hour ago is not a pass now.
  • Clear the board. Delete KAN-20 … KAN-43 — earlier rehearsals, including one over-grouped run. They make the board confusing on camera.
  • Start the listener. .venv/bin/python slack_listener.py and wait for ⚡️ Bolt app is running! The button beat is dead without it.
  • Arrange four windows before recording: editor on the transcript, terminal, Jira board, Slack. Never hunt for a window on camera.
  • Check audio for real. Record five seconds, play it back, listen for room noise and level. Do this before the take, not after.
  • Count the escalations. Exactly 2 is correct. Three means the on-call-dashboard item joined the thread — see below.
  • Postflight. test_grouping.py --check-db items.db grades what actually landed. No model calls, instant. Run it before you narrate anything.
  • Check the runtime against the event's stated limit before uploading.

If it drifts on camera

Three escalations instead of two. The on-call-dashboard item (KAN-52) has joined the thread. It is loud, not quiet: a third RECURRENCE ESCALATION fires, titles read [Recurring x5], and every member's count rewrites 4 → 5. The grouping is defensible either way — but the narration above says "occurrence four", so re-run rather than talk over it.

Fewer than two escalations. Read the extracted recurrence evidence first, not the adjudicator. If the sentence naming the earlier meetings was truncated, the whole group collapses and nothing logs an error.

An adapter fails mid-run. Keep going — per-adapter failure isolation is verified: the failing adapter errors alone and its peers still deliver. It is a decent thing to point out if it happens.
